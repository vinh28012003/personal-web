I have spent the last year building things that are smaller than the systems
they imitate, on purpose. A Redis server that is not Redis. A configuration
library that does one thing. The point was never to ship a product; it was to
find out which parts of a system I only thought I understood.

This is where that leaves me, and where I am pointing next.

## What I have actually built

Two projects, both deliberately scoped so the hard part was unavoidable:

- **Redis Lite**, a Redis server in C++ on a single-threaded epoll loop.
  375,000 pipelined operations per second, replication, and failover in under
  five seconds. I wrote about [what building it taught
  me](/blog/system-design-by-building).
- **cforge**, configuration injection into a running application in under 10
  milliseconds, no restart. Published to npm and PyPI.

And four engineering roles before that, the most recent as a Visiting Scholar
at the Kihara Lab at Purdue, where the work was less about throughput and more
about not breaking a pipeline other people depended on.

## The method I keep coming back to

Build the naive version, find its specific failure, read about *that*, rebuild.

It is slower than reading first. It is the only order in which the reading
sticks for me, because a paper about buffer management means very little until
you have watched one slow client stall an entire event loop.
